03.28
Chassis finalized. K12 scaffold. Six genes. Five boring, one loud. The
trick was not hiding the loud one. The trick was making the boring ones
unquestionably boring.

04.04
C. wants to encrypt before embedding. I keep telling him the code is
already a cipher to anyone who does not speak the language. Every amino
acid has its favourite spelling in this chassis. We simply choose a
different one. Same grammar. Different accent.

04.11
Scheme locked. We only touch redundancies that divide evenly. Where the
chassis offers exactly two spellings for a residue, or exactly four, we
spend. Anywhere else we stay silent. Greed is noise.

04.19
Ordering caused a fight. C. wanted the obvious one, which for him meant
ranking by how often the chassis picks each spelling. I refused. Adapt
to the chassis and any competent reader will adapt back. We use a
different order: the one a dictionary uses. Nothing else. If anyone
ever decodes this, they will hit the wall we sat at for a week before
we wrote that sentence down.

04.23
C. finally agreed to encrypt, on the condition that the key be something
no outsider could invent. I told him the organism itself is the key.
Not the noisy one, though. The quiet ones. Read them in the order they
appear on the chromosome. Each in its own direction, as the ribosome
would. Concatenate. Hash. Stretch. XOR. The payload travels dressed as
noise and nobody hears a word of it until you put its clothes back on.

04.27
Orientation: reverse strand. Not to hide. The truth reads backwards.
Sentimental. It stays.

05.02
Final sweep. No headers. No magic bytes. No shape-of-the-flag peeking
through. Until every step is right, the bytes are indistinguishable
from anything else random. Trust the process or do not start.

05.09
Shipping. One organism. One message. No backups.
